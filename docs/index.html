Prioritizing Content Decline Risk for Human Review

Evaluating whether a learned ranking model can concentrate potentially declining content pages near the top of a review queue more effectively than a transparent rule-based baseline.

30,000-row anonymized modeling dataset 32 pseudonymized clients 27 predictor features Client-grouped holdout evaluation

Title + Abstract

Question: Can observable content and SEO performance signals be used to prioritize pages that are labeled as declining for earlier human review?

Data: The research used the FlyRank internship warehouse to establish the data context and leakage considerations, while the reported Week 5–7 modeling experiments used a 30,000-row anonymized content-refresh dataset covering 32 pseudonymized clients.

Method: A hand-written ranking rule was compared with logistic regression using 27 engineered features and a client-grouped train/test split that held out seven previously unseen clients.

Result: On the held-out evaluation set, Precision@50 increased from 30% for the baseline to 74% for logistic regression, with 37 labeled declining pages in the model's top 50 compared with 15 under the baseline.

Use: The result supports using the model as a prioritization aid for human review, while not establishing causation, guaranteed future performance, or a case for fully automated content decisions.

1. Introduction / Problem

Content teams often have more pages to review than they can inspect in the same amount of time. A useful prioritization system should therefore help a reviewer decide which pages deserve attention first, without pretending that a model can replace the reviewer.

This research evaluates that decision-support problem. The central comparison is between a transparent hand-written baseline and a learned model that ranks pages by their estimated probability of belonging to the declining class.

Decision supported: If a team can review only a limited number of pages, which pages should receive earlier human attention?

The practical output is a ranked queue. The ranking is a prioritization mechanism, not an automated instruction to refresh, rewrite, redirect, or remove a page.

2. Data

Warehouse research foundation

The initial data investigation used the FlyRank internship warehouse. March 2026 was selected as the mid-panel development month for the Week 3 data-contract work rather than using the final month to develop label logic.

The March 2026 warehouse slice contained 9,841,378 rows and 30 columns, covering 2026-03-01 through 2026-03-31. This slice was used to verify the data grain, establish available signals, check availability, and investigate leakage considerations.

The final-month _sample data was treated separately from the mid-panel development analysis because the internship guidance identifies June 2026 as the final month and the natural outcome window for past-to-future label construction.

Modeling dataset

The reported Week 5–7 modeling experiments used the anonymized content_refresh_anonymized.csv dataset. It contains 30,000 content-level observations across 32 pseudonymized clients and 44 available fields.

The fields cover search demand, content characteristics, traffic, engagement, freshness, and search-performance signals. The final workflow used 27 predictor features.

Public-safe scope

Identifiers are pseudonymized. Public outputs contain derived model results, aggregate evaluation metrics, reason codes, and pseudonymized content identifiers only. No client names, private URLs, raw search queries, credentials, or other private information are included in this research artifact.

3. Methodology

Assumptions

The analysis assumes that observable page-level SEO and performance signals contain useful information for distinguishing pages labeled as declining from other pages. The objective is prioritization, not automatic decision-making, and the model does not establish that any individual feature causes decline.

Label definition

The supervised target, is_declining_label, was constructed from trend_direction: down was mapped to 1 and all other values to 0. Because trend_direction directly defines the target, it was excluded from the predictor set.

Features and preprocessing

The 27 predictors covered search demand and competition, CPC, content size and characteristics, impressions, clicks, sessions, AI sessions, days with impressions and sessions, content age and freshness, CTR, average search position, engagement rate, scroll rate, AI traffic percentage, and categorical tiers for competition, content type, intent, age, freshness, content size, impressions, and position.

Highly skewed traffic variables — impressions, clicks, sessions, and AI sessions — were transformed using log1p. Numerical missing values were median-imputed and standardized; categorical missing values were replaced with the most frequent category and categorical variables were one-hot encoded. Preprocessing was kept inside the modeling pipeline.

Baseline

A transparent hand-written rule was established before supervised modeling. It provided a practical benchmark representing a simple signal-based prioritization approach. The baseline and learned model were compared using the same evaluation framework.

Validation design

The modeling workflow used a client-grouped train/test split. 25 clients were used for training and 7 previously unseen clients were held out for testing. GroupShuffleSplit used test_size=0.20 and random_state=42, with client_id as the grouping variable. Training and test client sets were checked for overlap.

Model and evaluation

The primary supervised model was logistic regression. Its predicted probability for the declining class was used as the ranking score. Pages were ordered from highest to lowest score rather than relying only on the default 0.5 classification threshold.

Evaluation included accuracy, precision, recall, and Precision@50. Precision@50 is the operationally relevant metric here because it measures the proportion of labeled declining pages among the 50 pages receiving the highest review priority.

Leakage checks

trend_direction, trend_pct, and is_declining_label were excluded because they contain or directly define outcome information. content_id was excluded as an identifier rather than a predictive signal, and client_id was reserved for grouped validation.

The earlier warehouse-stage leakage exercise deliberately introduced a label-derived variable, observed the resulting misleading score improvement, and removed it from the honest workflow. This was used as a practical check that outcome information was not being allowed into the final predictors.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

The results show stronger ranking performance than the tested baseline under the stated evaluation setup. They do not establish production performance, causal relationships, or the correctness of every individual recommended action.

Dataset scope

The reported model results come from the 30,000-row anonymized content-refresh dataset, not the full FlyRank warehouse.

Label limitation

The target represents the dataset's definition of a downward trend. A high score does not independently establish that a page is objectively underperforming.

Causality

Observed associations do not show that changing an individual feature will prevent or reverse decline.

Validation scope

The evaluation uses one client-grouped holdout: 25 training clients and 7 test clients. Precision@50 is an observed result for this setup, not a guarantee.

Operational context

Strategic importance, seasonality, technical issues, planned changes, and other context may not be represented in the features.

Monitoring

Performance should be re-evaluated if ranking quality declines, important feature distributions shift, the declining-page proportion changes materially, or new clients/content types appear outside the training population.

Human review remains mandatory. The model is decision support. A high-risk page should receive attention sooner; it should not automatically be refreshed, rewritten, redirected, or removed.

6. Ranked Recommendations

The model's main operational output is a ranked review queue. The ranking determines where limited review capacity should be spent first; it does not determine the final content action.

  1. Review high-risk pages first. Start with the pages receiving the highest decline-risk scores.
  2. Prioritize high-risk pages that are also stale. Confirm whether the page remains strategically relevant and whether a refresh is appropriate.
  3. Investigate low CTR before changing content. Review search-result presentation, intent alignment, and relevance before selecting an intervention.
  4. Investigate search position and engagement signals. Treat these as review prompts rather than automatic diagnoses.
  5. Use lower-ranked pages as secondary work. Continue down the queue according to capacity and business priorities.

Recommended operating workflow

Rank → Review → Diagnose → Decide → Act → Monitor

Model ranking and rule-based reason codes serve different purposes. The model prioritizes pages; the reason codes summarize observable conditions that a reviewer can inspect. They are not causal explanations of the logistic-regression prediction.
